checks#

Replaying a published claim through OCP and reporting what the model says about it.

A trail claim is checked by four functions, cheapest first. What separates them is not the question each asks – each says that itself – but two mechanical choices, what gets pinned and what the solver is then asked for:

feasible pin the claim ask for any solution weight pin the claim demand the claimed weight optimal pin nothing search for the optimum unique pin nothing search, forbid that optimum, search again

The pinned two stay cheap at high round counts because the trail fixes nearly every variable; the searching two explore the whole space and are realistic only for few rounds.

A bound claim has no trail to pin, so it takes the other path: search_optimum for the round count in question, then compare_bound against the claimed relation. That is one full search per round, which is what makes replaying a bounds table expensive – a ten-row table is ten searches, not one.

max_feasible_prefix is the diagnostic when a claim comes back infeasible: how far the assignments can be pinned before the model refuses, which separates three causes that otherwise look identical – the paper is wrong, we transcribed it wrong, or our round/layer mapping is off.

recheck.checks.max_feasible_prefix(spec, cipher=None, model_type='milp', config_solver=None)[source]#

How many of the claim’s assignments can be pinned before the model turns infeasible.

Equal to the number of assignments when the whole claim is feasible; otherwise the index of the first one that cannot hold, which is where to look for the transcription error.

recheck.checks.search_key(spec, rounds)[source]#

What a bound search actually depends on, so identical searches can be shared.

Several papers state the same optimum, and each is a separate claim file. The search behind them is one and the same: it is fixed by the primitive, the round count and the goal, and knows nothing of who claimed what. Batch runners key a cache on this.

recheck.checks.check_bound(spec, model_type='milp', config_solver=None, rounds=None)[source]#

Search the optimum for each round the claim covers and test the stated relation.

A bound claim carries no trail: it says what the best value over r rounds is ("="), or at least is (">=", how design documents usually phrase a security argument), or at most is ("<=", a trail was found but optimality was not proven). Nothing is pinned, so every round is a full search – pass rounds to check only part of the table.
